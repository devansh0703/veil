Before it quotes, the rail reads the mint and the destination and confirms both can hold a confidential transfer. If either cannot be checked, it offers nothing.
2 · where the proofs come from
The payer's machine, with no service in the loop.
equality
the balance written is the old balance minus this amount
validity
both grouped ciphertexts really encrypt that amount
range
every committed value is in range, so nothing wraps negative
All three are built by WASM inside the Solana ZK SDK, before any network call — an offline wallet can still author a payment. Roughly 50 ms for all three in Node.

It never downgrades to a public payment.

A refusal carries no accepts[] entry at all — an offer alongside a refusal would invite a client to pay for something the rail has already said it cannot settle privately.
5 · the graph layer
One-time seats, never re-armed.
256
pre-armed one-time accounts on devnet
0
seats ever re-used — re-arming would link two payments
2
payments to one merchant, two unrelated accounts
A seat cannot be derived — there is one deterministic address form per owner and mint — so the pool is created once and its addresses recorded. What is deterministic is which slot a payment takes, so the merchant reconciles without the payer telling it anything.

Veil. A technical walkthrough of a confidential x402 rail on Solana.
An agent asks for a resource. Unpaid, it gets a standard 402: a price, a one-time account, and a privacy claim it can check. Before quoting, the rail reads the mint and the destination, confirming both can hold a confidential transfer.
A confidential transfer needs three proofs: ciphertext-commitment equality, batched ciphertext validity, and a range proof over the remaining balance. All three are built on the payer's own machine, by WASM inside the Solana ZK SDK. No prover service, no fee, about fifty milliseconds.
The transfer is Token-2022, instruction family twenty-seven, operation seven. Solana's own token program verifies those proofs on chain. The payer signs the money movement; the rail co-signs only the network fee. The merchant decrypts its own recipient ciphertext with its own wallet key — one key per merchant.
Every refusal has one code, one cause, one remedy. The load-bearing one is VEIL-CONF-003: if the rail cannot demonstrate that a payment will be confidential, it refuses and offers nothing. A refusal carries no accepts entry at all.
Confidential balances hide the amount, not that an account exists. So every payment lands in a different one-time account from a pre-created pool. Seats are never re-armed — reusing one would link two payments on the public graph.
The claim is about someone else's blockchain, so it should not be taken on our word. Replay reads the chain, not our ledger, and asserts seven things: the transaction exists, it succeeded, it carries exactly one confidential transfer, and no readable amount moved.
Three things landed in 2026. Batch settlement: capital committed once, a signed voucher per call, and a confidential redemption — only the session total reaches the chain. One-transaction settlement behind txv1. And V2 headers.
What stays public, stated plainly: the account addresses, the mint, and the timing. That is the limit of this primitive, and it is on our limits page in the same words.
