## Problem Statement

Veil ships a working confidential x402 rail: 64 seats per alias on devnet + testnet, real hosted settlements on both clusters, CI green, two real payments this session. Three things changed in the world since that design was approved, and none of our docs knew about them:

1. **x402 Batch Settlement (May 11, 2026, official protocol scheme).** Buyers commit capital once; every HTTP interaction carries a signed cumulative voucher ("I owe you"); the seller verifies by signature math with zero chain lookups and serves instantly; value moves on-chain in bulk when economically optimal. This is exactly the payment-channel shape Veil reinvented with pre-armed seats — except Veil's seat is consumed permanently per payment while a batch session amortizes across "hundreds or thousands of interactions."
2. **Single-transaction confidential transfers (SIMD-0385, txv1 gate).** The llm4agents audit (2026-09-19) showed confidential transfers used to need 3-4 dependent transactions (proof records, context accounts, cleanup). txv1 raises max transaction size to 4,096 bytes, folding proof verification into one transaction. Verified live on devnet this session: gate `txv1aq4pp281K9um3tnPgkfX8UqtFT6wcVW3hNezGLL` ACTIVE at slot 493,742,080 (chain now at ~507.6M). Solana mainnet activated txv1 Sep 15, 2026. Veil's current payer flow still runs the legacy multi-transaction proof-record dance.
3. **Stock x402 facilitators cannot verify confidential transfers — by design.** The SVM exact scheme requires a readable `TransferChecked` with a plaintext amount and a public balance delta. A confidential transfer fails all three structurally (different discriminator, ciphertext instruction data, zero public balance delta). This is Veil's moat, but it is also now documented publicly — judges and competitors have read the same audit.

Also relevant: x402 V2 (Dec 2025) moved payment data to `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` / `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` / `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` headers; Veil still speaks V1 `X-PAYMENT`. Reference SDKs are V2-era (`@x402/core` 2.28.0). And confidential liquidity is thin: PYUSD/USDG mints carry the extension with `auto_approve=false` (issuer approval required per account), USDC/USDT/EURC are plain SPL mints that cannot do confidential transfers at all — the only route is wrapping.

## What Makes This Cool

Veil's product thesis just got external validation from a third-party audit of the ecosystem: the one thing stock x402 cannot do — verify a payment it cannot read — is the thing Veil was built to do. The plan below turns Veil from "a demo that hides amounts" into the confidential rail for the batched-payment era: sessions instead of per-payment seats, one transaction instead of a proof-record dance, V2 headers instead of deprecated ones.

## Research verification (2026-10-05 pass)

The first draft of this plan was written from partially stale research. A full re-research pass verified every claim against dated primary sources and found four things no Veil doc had:

- **The batch-settlement spec is chain-abstract, and SVM is a supported network — corrected 2026-10-12.** The spec mandates a per-network binding (commitment format, verification, double-spend prevention, expiry, redemption, trust model) and defines capital-backed vs credit-backed trust. The Oct 5 pass read the FAQ's "on EVM" line as the whole story; **it is not.** Upstream publishes four bindings — generic, Cloudflare, EVM, and (since 2026-08-21) **SVM** — and the SVM one is not confidential: payment-channels escrow, an Ed25519 voucher the chain verifies through the precompile, a **public** `settled` watermark, and Token-2022 only as a mint owner. So F9 is not "write the missing binding" — it is **write the confidential-redemption sibling to the binding that exists**, and say in the same breath that it is not wire-compatible with it. Sources and the blob sha are in `docs/notes/x402-2026-landscape.md` N1; the divergences are `docs/notes/svm-batch-binding.md` §0.
- **Five published attacks on x402 (arXiv 2605.11781, May 12, 2026)**, 25k-request testbed, validated live (248 grants from one payment; caches leaking paid content on 100% of tested nginx configs). Each attack has a published mitigation; two of them map to gaps Veil has today — those are F7 and F8.
- **Three confidential-x402 efforts exist, all on EVM:** TACEO Merces (Base Sepolia, confidential scheme, live demo, May 2026), PrivateX402 (Nethermind research post, Feb 2026 — proofs in a TEE today), Fhenix (FHE; blog Oct 2025, privacy-stages press Jan 2026). None on Solana. **Do not read this as "nobody else has privacy":** they do, and each delegates it to something you must trust — an MPC operator set, a TEE, an FHE coprocessor. The defensible position is not the word privacy, it is that our claim is re-derivable from chain state by a tool we ship (`npm run replay`, 7 assertions). The may/may-not-claim register is `docs/notes/x402-2026-landscape.md` N7; it exists because a claim about another team's product is a factual claim.
- **Facilitator concentration is now a measured market fact** (SSRN 7396241, Aug 2026): settlement routes concentrate in a handful of facilitators. Veil's self-hostable verifier stops being a DX nicety and becomes the trust-architecture answer.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Deliverables-first, protocol research as documentation only
Close every remaining deliverable against the current rail, publish the 2026 landscape as docs (docs/notes/x402-2026-landscape.md), and defer all protocol work post-submission.
- Effort: S-M. Risk: Low. Reuses: everything shipped.
- Pros: zero regression risk before the deadline; every scored criterion gets its artifact.
- Cons: the submission misses the single biggest 2026 story (batch settlement) and ships a multi-transaction flow judges can now compare against a one-tx standard.

### Approach B: Protocol parity sprint, deliverables compressed
Migrate to V2 headers + batch-session scheme + txv1 flow first; fold the deliverables (video, smoke, docs) around the new architecture.
- Effort: L. Risk: High — regressing a green rail 7 days before close is how submissions die. Reuses: @x402 2.28.0 already V2-era.
- Pros: the demo shows the 2026 protocol, not the 2025 one.
- Cons: batch settlement has no confidential-reference implementation; building one under deadline pressure risks both the new scheme and the old deliverables.

### Approach C: Dual-track — deliverables on the stable rail, 2026 features as additive schemes behind flags (RECOMMENDED, APPROVED)
Keep the deployed rail byte-stable as the demo substrate. Add the 2026 work as new, additive capabilities that never touch existing endpoints: (1) `batch-confidential` scheme = session escrow + signed vouchers + on-chain bulk settlement into one-time seats; (2) txv1 single-tx payment path for the payer SDK; (3) V2 header support alongside V1; (4) liquidity/mint-topology doc grounded in the audited reality; (5) the attack-surface hardening set (F7/F8) and the SVM binding spec (F9). Deliverables land first on the stable rail; protocol features land behind explicit scheme negotiation so a judge sees both stories: "what ships today" and "where this goes."
- Effort: M. Risk: Low-Med — additive only, feature-flagged, the green rail never changes. Reuses: PoolLedger, reserve/CAS store, refusal CATALOG, derive package, hosted rails, CI gates.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C.** It is the only path where the deadline deliverables and the 2026 story both ship without gambling one for the other. The rail that already settles real payments stays untouched; everything new is additive and negotiated by scheme, which is exactly how x402 V2 says extensions should work.

## The 2026 Feature Plan (all additive — nothing existing is modified or removed)

### F1 — `batch-confidential` scheme (the headline)
A session is capital committed once; each HTTP interaction carries a cumulative signed voucher; the merchant verifies vouchers offline (no chain lookups on the hot path — this is the "speed of HTTP" claim the x402 batch-settlement post makes, now with amounts hidden); the merchant redeems the session's final voucher as ONE confidential transfer into a fresh one-time seat from the existing pool. Privacy upgrade over stock batch settlement: per-call usage is a voucher signature, invisible on-chain; only the session total ever lands on-chain, encrypted, readable by the merchant alone. Escrow semantics follow the x402 batch spec: explicit limits, signed commitments, defined refund/withdrawal exit ramps.
- **Spec conformance (new in this revision):** the batch spec is chain-abstract and mandates a per-network binding. F9 authors the SVM binding; F1 implements against it — commitment format, verification rules, storage behavior, double-spend prevention (PoolLedger CAS), expiry, redemption mechanism, and trust model (**capital-backed**: pre-armed seats are the committed escrow, no intermediary underwrites). This makes F1 the first conformant SVM binding, not an ad-hoc composition.
- Files: `packages/server/src/index.ts` (new scheme registration, additive), `packages/client/src/index.ts` (session open/charge/close), `packages/derive` (session-id → seat derivation, reusing the existing one-way derivation), docs/api.md (new scheme section).
- Open question: session escrow on devnet with free tiers — likely a USDT-wrapped-minor escrow account held by the payer with a signed spend-cap the merchant can enforce at redemption, not a custodial pool. Decided during implementation against the x402 batch docs (docs.x402.org/schemes/batch-settlement).

### F2 — txv1 single-transaction settlement path
Collapse the payer's proof-record flow (write proofs → verify → close records) into one ≤4,096-byte transaction, gated on runtime feature-gate detection (probe `txv1aq4pp281K9um3tnPgkfX8UqtFT6wcVW3hNezGLL`; devnet active at slot 493,742,080, mainnet active since Sep 15, 2026). Legacy path stays as fallback for pre-txv1 clusters — zero deletion.
- Files: `packages/client/src/index.ts`, `packages/onchain/src/index.ts` (single-tx instruction assembly), scripts/pay-live.ts (new mode flag), docs (txv1 note).

### F3 — x402 V2 header support, additive
Accept/emit `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` / `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` / `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` alongside V1 `X-PAYMENT`. Facilitator negotiates: V2 if the client offers it, V1 otherwise. The hosted demo keeps working exactly as-is.
- Files: `packages/server/src/index.ts`, `packages/client/src/index.ts`, refusal-check coverage for both header styles.

### F4 — Confidentiality verification layer as a product (the moat, named)
Publicize the standing result: stock facilitators structurally cannot verify confidential payments (audit-backed). Veil's verifier asserts proof inclusion + merchant-only decryption. Ship it as the explicit differentiator in docs + landing copy, backed by the hostile-QA test (deliverable D-6) proving the claim is enforced, not asserted.
- **Competitive framing (new in this revision):** TACEO Merces, PrivateX402, and Fhenix all ship confidential-x402 work — all on EVM. None on Solana. Their own "what stays public" tables (addresses, frequency, timing) match Veil's honest-limits page exactly. The landing copy can now say "the confidential facilitator for Solana" with three dated citations behind it, and the SSRN concentration finding (Aug 2026) turns `veil verify` / standalone artifacts into the trust counter to facilitator concentration.

### F5 — Liquidity & mint-topology reality doc
`docs/notes/confidential-liquidity-2026.md`: what the audit showed (PYUSD/USDG auto_approve=false — Paxos/PayPal issuer approval per account; USDC/USDT/EURC not extension-capable at all; wrap-mint route), what it means for Veil (self-issued test-dollar mint is the right demo call — now with competitive evidence: TACEO's demo also runs on its own confidential token), and the mint-per-merchant vs shared-mint decision inputs (feeds TODOS T-4).

### F6 — Session receipts (from the user's earlier reconciliation thread)
Batch sessions produce a per-session receipt: session id, voucher chain head, final amount (merchant-decrypted), settled seat. This is the reconciliation artifact that makes batched confidential volume auditable by the merchant without exposing it publicly — the missing piece between "per-payment rows" (shipped) and "session economics."

### F7 — Resource-bound payments + replay hardening (NEW — arXiv attack II)
The five-attack paper's headline live result: one captured payment replayed for **248 grants**. Veil's CAS seat ledger already prevents seat reuse — the strongest existing defense in the paper's taxonomy — but the binding is seat-level, not resource-level. This feature closes the remaining gap:
- Every signed payment/voucher carries the **resource path it pays for**; the facilitator refuses a payment presented against a different resource (refusal-catalog entry, not a crash).
- Every response to a paid request carries an **idempotency key** (the payment signature), so a retried HTTP request is answered deterministically instead of double-granting.
- Regression tests replay a captured payment against (a) the same resource, (b) a different resource, (c) a different session — asserting REFUSE or idempotent-replay behavior in each case.
- Files: `packages/client` (sign resource into payload), `packages/server` (verify + refusal entry), refusal-check + tests, docs/api.md.

### F8 — Cache hygiene for paid responses (NEW — arXiv attack III)
The paper leaked paid content through misconfigured caches (100% of tested nginx configs, live CDN). Published mitigation: `Cache-Control: no-store` on paid responses. Veil's exposure is currently "partial — needs an audit + test." This feature:
- Audits every response path that returns paid content (and the 402 body itself) for caching headers.
- Sets `no-store` (plus `Vary` where relevant) on all of them.
- Adds a regression test asserting the header on a granted paid GET — so a future handler that forgets it fails CI.
- Files: `packages/server` response path, tests, docs/api.md (caching contract note).

### F9 — Author the SVM confidential-redemption binding for x402 batch settlement (corrected: the slot is company, not empty)
Publish `docs/notes/svm-batch-binding.md`: an SVM binding that covers the seven things the spec mandates **and** specifies a redemption upstream's SVM binding does not have — a Token-2022 confidential transfer (§0 of the note states every divergence, including that the two are not wire-compatible). Contents mandated by the spec: commitment format (signed cumulative voucher over session id + sequence + amount), verification rules (offline signature check, sequence monotonicity), storage behavior (merchant-side session state; no hot-path chain lookups), double-spend prevention (PoolLedger CAS + one redemption per session), expiry (session TTL that refuses late charges — and, stated as a limit rather than a feature, no refund path: nothing was charged before redemption, so there is nothing to reverse), redemption mechanism (single confidential transfer into a pooled one-time seat, txv1 single-tx when the gate is live), and trust model (**capital-backed** — pre-armed seats are committed escrow; no intermediary underwrites).
- Why it's in the plan: it is what makes F1 legible to a standards reader (a binding shaped like the spec's own checklist, not a fork), the confidential redemption is the part upstream has not specified, and it is cheap — a spec-shaped doc plus a conformance checklist F1's tests assert against. The novelty claim is *the confidential redemption*, not the binding; the earlier "genuinely unclaimed" line was based on a finding that has since been corrected (N1).
- Files: `docs/notes/svm-batch-binding.md`, conformance checklist consumed by F1 tests, referenced from docs/api.md and the landscape register.
